# Navigation preparation

The renderer resolves and pins reusable owners before dispatch. The same
validated decision is consumed during adoption. Canonical ground has an exact
terrain/compiler recipe and sampled terrain, coast and river proofs; roads,
city appearance and forest exclusions belong to object preparation. Ground and
objects use separate jobs, backing kinds and GPU owners in the existing cache
and worker pool. Legacy compilation keeps its combined path.

Required keys control retirement; urgency controls order. Retargeting removes
obsolete pending/ready work and discards obsolete active completion after its
owned input lease ends. A full ready reservoir has one bounded, separately
charged 16 MiB demanded-result handoff on an existing lane. Configured worker,
ready-reservoir, geometry and backing budgets remain unchanged.

GPU-ready results publish before optional persistence. After adoption, detached
CPU snapshots use at most 16 MiB, four queued callbacks and one existing worker
lane. Saturation, contention and fragmentation skip optional writes; they do
not compact the file. Explicit backing-only preparation retains synchronous
persistence and validates active survivors before declaring success.

Verification: host component, proof, exact-key, queue, lifetime and assembly
contracts pass. Windows backing tests cover concurrent reads/writes,
fragmentation, cancellation and retirement. The native road-only edit leaves
ground compilation and uploads unchanged while compiling 11 object components.
Pan, clock, zoom and wrap add no ground compilation after entering content is
prepared.

Strict pixel qualification remains open. All corresponding native depth
captures match R9; sparse color differences and the separate initial-origin
outlier are tracked independently. Reference images and visual acceptance are
unchanged.

The original full busy route completes all 30 requests, including 29 exact
source/generation/workload destination Presents and one deliberately superseded
request. Maximum acceptance-to-Present is 1.646 seconds within the original
deadline. At all 27 post-adoption queue observations, required, expected and
consumed cardinalities match, with zero unused ready bytes, recovery or needed
result evictions. These drained observations do not measure peak lane occupancy.
The normal ready reservoir peaks at 54.15 MiB; the separate handoff is unused.

R9 times out at destination 26, so its 24 matching completed endpoints provide
diagnostics only. First-visit latency changes from 6.956 to 1.604 seconds;
ordinary scroll remains about one second. The short busy pair qualifies 11 of
12 destinations: mean scroll changes from 942 to 913 ms, zoom from 762 to 827 ms,
and startup from 53.275 to 50.605 seconds. Reverse-return has different actual
unit membership and is excluded. The light pair qualifies all 12 destinations:
scroll changes from 631 to 551 ms, zoom from 527 to 542 ms, and startup from
17.297 to 16.985 seconds. Idle cadence is essentially unchanged. These single
observations establish no general warm-navigation speedup.

The accepted runtime, saves, configuration, link, cursor and environment are
restored and hash-verified; owned test processes and tasks are closed. Cleanup
removes 375.8 MB of hash-identical guest copies and rebuildable intermediates,
preserving unique inputs, matching binaries and current/control defect evidence.
