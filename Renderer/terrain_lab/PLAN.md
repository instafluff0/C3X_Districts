# Renderer Lab Plan

The Renderer Lab is the standalone visual and asset-development path for every
map-rendered system, not only terrain. Every planned visual system is made
correct and visually acceptable here before its own result is promoted to Game
Integration. Each gate changes one visual system, preserves the previous pass
for A/B comparison, and must remain continuous across the test scene. The
historical `terrain_lab/` directory name remains to avoid churn in executable and
evidence paths.

The lab owns asset extraction/normalization, mappings, geometry, materials,
lighting, animation, effects, scale, grounding, readability, and art direction.
It does not own Civ III capture, caches, redraw scheduling, scrolling/wrapping
behavior, native sprite suppression, compositing, retained overlays, or runtime
fallback; those belong to Game Integration. The shared boundary and handoff
record are defined in [../docs/renderer_workstreams.md](../docs/renderer_workstreams.md).

## Promotion rule

L9's accepted historical promotion render contains 48 contiguous Civ III tiles in an 8x6 grid; L10 and L11 preserve their accepted 96-tile, 12x8 evidence. Beginning with L12 and continuing through every subsequent lab gate, each step ends with a promotion render containing **at least 192 contiguous Civ III tiles**. The required fixture is a 16x12 parallelogram whose two local axes each follow a true Civ III edge-adjacency direction, producing a diamond-shaped map patch rather than a rectangular stack of screen rows. The scene must include the newly added system, every previously accepted terrain and object layer, and a useful mixture of the terrain families those layers touch. Every cell must remain individually legible at Civ III's 2:1 diamond scale; the map must exercise shared edges, blends, depth, shadows, repeated terrain families, and multiple nontrivial adjacencies or overlaps instead of becoming disconnected swatches. Forest, jungle, mountain, and other mutually exclusive tile features must have explicit cell assignments; feature bodies may overhang their own cell naturally but must not be composited over incompatible relief in another cell.

Generate the promotion scene at native inspection size plus a reduced readability view, preserve the preceding accepted render for A/B comparison, and provide useful layer-isolation views when the new system can obscure an earlier one. From L12 onward, every 192-tile promotion fixture must be a deterministic 16-by-12 true-adjacency diamond viewport selected dynamically from an authoritative installed GOG BIQ on the Windows VM through C3X Editor's read-only BIQ parser. Prefer `Conquests/Scenarios/test.biq` when it contains the current system; otherwise use an installed Firaxis scenario BIQ that does, record why, and never edit or synthesize its terrain cells. Preserve the selected raw BIQ coordinates and tile semantics as local evidence. Tile ownership remains exact while ground materials, compatible relief, and shore envelopes blend continuously across shared edges. When the selected viewport lacks a map-object family required by the current gate, preserve all BIQ terrain cells and native fields unchanged and place the missing objects in a separate deterministic, source-independent Lab-only scenario layer. Record that augmentation explicitly and never describe it as captured Civ III state. Connected systems such as roads and railroads must use dense player-like networks with long runs, loops, branches, junctions, intentional dead ends, crossings, relief traversal, and wrap continuations rather than isolated decorative samples. Record deterministic inputs and output hashes, but do not treat them as visual acceptance. The user must explicitly approve the promotion render before work begins on the next lab gate; lack of approval remains a pending visual checkpoint rather than implied acceptance.

## Gates

1. **L0 — Material truth: complete.** Render the grass base color through the real DDS/sRGB/filtering path. The source swatch and flat patch must agree without grading or displacement.
2. **L1 — Micro material response: complete.** Add only material-height normals and restrained specular response. These channels may change light response but never the silhouette.
3. **L2 — Broad form: complete.** Displace one densely subdivided surface with a low-frequency generic heightfield and calculate geometric normals from that same field. No authored terrain element is admitted yet.
4. **L3 — Shape grounding: complete.** Add deterministic directional self-shadow and broad contact/valley occlusion derived from the continuous macro heightfield. Preserve an unshadowed relief pass for direct comparison.
5. **L4 — Authored hill intake: complete.** Display every candidate hill height channel as source truth, then replace the generic heightfield with exactly one validated hill family. Reject assets that create per-tile domes, seams, or unreadable scale.
6. **L5 — Authored mountain intake: complete.** Test one mountain variant and its footprint/blend/region channels on a flat/hill/mountain fixture. Require a range-like footprint, material zoning, geometric normals, and grounded shadows before adding variants.
7. **L6 — Coast topology: complete through accepted L9.5/L9 promotion.** The original shared contour was technically continuous but visually too clean and strip-like. L9.5 adds independent, restrained roughness to the land/beach width, waterline, and surf envelope while keeping all bands connected. Cliff and rock dressing remain intentionally out of scope for the current beach gate.
8. **L7 — Water stack: complete through accepted L9.5/L9 promotion.** The standard Civ VI coast recipe is traced to Beach, Shallows, `Water/Coast`, and the wave settings. The shallow bed slopes continuously from the beach into the authored low-depth range; transparent coast water uses the normalized density ramps, three LEAN scales, tiling/non-tiling normal and variance pairs, gloss/mask channels, deterministic bed refraction, and authored Fresnel/specular constants; surf uses the extracted crash-foam, ripple, and turbulence channels.
9. **L8 — Authored forest and jungle intake: complete.** The fail-closed adapter accounts for all 33 unique entries in the installed base-forest, snow-forest, and jungle sets: 22 real desktop vegetation bodies are normalized with their material textures and ArtDef placement records, while 11 rock/decal/fallback entries are explicitly routed or excluded. Native and thumbnail contact sheets validate two views of every accepted body at common relative scale. Proxy vegetation is not accepted.
10. **L9 — Terrain beauty scene: complete; 48-tile promotion approved.** L9.3 and L9.4 remain comparison baselines. L9.5 corrects the over-clean shore with a smooth material-height-eroded grass/sand transition, variable beach width, a separately perturbed surf contour, submerged beach continuation, a continuous shallow-bed slope, visible authored underwater detail, and non-foam surface rippling. It deliberately excludes unrelated beach-blanket/FOW, flood-submergence, ocean-decal, cliff, and rock assets from the standard beach recipe. The approved native 2048x1280 promotion scene is an explicit 8x6 grid of Civ III-proportioned diamonds: six dense forest cells and six dense jungle cells in mutually exclusive blocks, two taller cell-local authored mountains separated from vegetation by a clear column, eight authored hill cells, plains, desert, a six-cell coast, and sea. Complete, no-vegetation, no-water, no-surf, and 512x320 thumbnail views are preserved.
11. **L10 — Desert dunes: complete; 96-tile promotion approved.** Use the confirmed procedural dune controls, desert-hills material, and five sand height decals inventoried in [DUNE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md](DUNE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) to create broad, directional, tile-continuous dune waves while preserving small sand ripples as material detail. The approved 96-tile candidate contains one connected 4x4 dune field with strongly meandering S-curved crests, separate flat desert and twelve authored desert/plain hills, a source-material desert mountain, plains and coast transitions, and the complete accepted L9 stack. Its shoreline retains natural bays and points while centering the land/naval transition near the x=10 grid boundary for clear tile ownership. Complete, no-dunes, dunes-only, and 640x360 views are preserved.
12. **L11 — Marshes: complete; 96-tile promotion approved.** Render wet ground, shallow water, mud, reeds, grasses, and sparse vegetation as one terrain-integrated system with convincing depth, clustering, seasonal response, and transitions into grassland, coast, and river edges. Use the normalized Civ VI `GrassMarsh` material and all reachable `CLUTTER_MARSH` projected decals according to their audited ArtDef placement records; do not invent a marsh vegetation mesh when the source set defines none. The accepted viewport is locked to raw BIQ origin `(53,55)` and includes a two-cell adjacency halo. The user explicitly approved the corrected continuous shoreline and marsh composition on 2026-09-05.
13. **L12 — Volcanoes: complete; 192-tile promotion approved.** The accepted scene renders the single normalized Civ VI ordinary-volcano terrain element and its dormant/active material channels directly, with rigid orientation plus bounded Civ III footprint/aspect and vertical calibration providing deterministic instance variation. Edge-adjacent mountain and volcano cells overlap their authored footprints in world space so ranges form connected shoulders and saddles rather than isolated cones; one outer envelope fades both displacement and mountain material smoothly into surrounding grassland. Hills and authored relief flatten through the same continuous signed-shore field before reaching the beach, and mountain-to-hill boundaries merge through a narrow grass-covered shoulder. The accepted shared stack also uses dense deterministic normalized vegetation and restrained projected ocean, coast, grassland, plains, dark-plains, and grassland-hill clutter. ## Promotion and final gate

Each system may move to Game Integration as soon as its own lab technical gate
and required visual approval are complete. Promotion freezes a handoff record
covering the pack/definition/scene versions, logical assets, supported states,
reference render and hash, ownership intent, cache-relevant inputs, update needs,
and complete custom-coverage boundary. The same-numbered `I#` gate consumes that
record: L12 feeds I12, L13 feeds I13, and so on. Integration may proceed while
the lab advances to the next gate.

L21 remains the final combined beauty-scene and release-level visual gate. It
proves that all accepted systems compose well together, but it does not delay
integration of a previously approved system. Promotion never transfers runtime
ownership by itself. Before its I# gate a new category is intentionally absent
from the custom-on map plane; after its I# gate it is custom-owned and must draw
or make the frame fail visibly. It never silently falls back to native map art.
