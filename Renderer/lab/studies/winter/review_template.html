Towns, farms and roads

Ancient towns on test.biq through the Lab tile overlays, which draw the map's own pine forests as the game does. Roofs and ledges take a snow coat as bright as the ground's, mottled at building scale with a few bare dabs where the roof shows through; walls keep their timber and plaster, so towns stay legible. Farm plots keep their shapes: snow settles in the furrows and each plot reads as fallow, ploughed or stubble.

A later era

A window of the 1498 AD save: industrial towns, dense roads and rails, irrigation everywhere, mountains and a bay. Roads become trodden grey-brown tracks rather than dark lines, so the network stays readable without dominating the snow.

Evening and night

No new lights. The existing day cycle on snow: warm low sun against blue shade at 18:00, moonlit snow and glowing windows at midnight.

Snow flurries over mountains

A gentle flurry of small flakes falls only over mountain tiles, drawn by Renderer64 itself in the per-frame effect layer that animates chimney smoke and volcano plumes: one quad stands on each mountain tile, and three parallax layers of flakes drift down it, fading in above the peak and out near the ground. Built in a private Lab tree; nothing in the checkout changed.

Coast, Sea and Ocean stay apart

Players must be able to tell the three water types apart. Each tile's centre is sampled on a stretch of test.biq with wide bands of all three; separation is the colour distance between the class means (about 2 is barely noticeable, 5 and up is clear).

{{water_rows}}
RenderCoast–SeaSea–OceanCoast–Ocean

Production today cannot tell Sea from Ocean in any season: the water's depth tint follows distance from shore only and is the same beyond about two tiles. Winter now darkens open water smoothly with distance and adds a mild step from each tile's own Civ III class, so a Sea tile far from land still reads as Sea. A step from the class alone separates them cleanly but shows the tiles' diamonds:

A smooth and exact version needs one production change: carry each tile corner's class blend in the water's vertex data, so the shader interpolates classes across borders the way it already interpolates depth.

Desert, Plains, Grassland and Tundra stay apart

One snow over everything made the four open land types identical. Grassland stays a clean, smooth white; the others differ through the snow itself rather than bare patches: plains carry a scatter of small gold stubble flecks, desert snow is a gentle cream shaped by dune ripples, and tundra lies under a cooler blue-grey snow with a wind-crusted surface. The type weights blend continuously, so borders stay soft. Texture carries much of the difference, so the colour numbers below understate it.

Plains and grassland are the subtlest pair: the same white, told apart by the plains' fine stipple of stubble rather than by colour. More flecks or a warmer plains snow would separate them further, at some cost to the clean look.

Forests

Your picks. Broadleaf crowns carry snow on their upper leaves over darker shaded foliage. Pine forests switch to the snowy-pine variety Renderer64 already ships (same meshes, authored snow). Production uses it only for pines on tundra today, so choosing it in Winter is one small production change; the Lab previews exactly that change behind a switch in a private build.

Before any of this reaches Renderer64

  • The frame season is already in frame.season; production needs it in a shader constant with these functions behind it. Today the Lab's private shader root is the winter variant.
  • Foliage classes (broadleaf, pine, snowy pine) come from atlas colour in the Lab. Production should read a pack flag, never names.
  • The relief draw has no snow texture bound, so ground snow is procedural. Binding the pack's ground snow material would give a richer crust.
  • The mountain flurry is one new effect kind plus an emitter per mountain tile in both object paths, gated on Winter instead of the Lab switch. Every view with a mountain then redraws continuously, as a smoking city already does.
  • Coast, Sea and Ocean: a per-corner class blend in the water's vertex data would make the class step smooth and exact, in every season.
  • Not attempted yet: frozen lake sheets, season transitions, frost on units.
  • These are Lab previews on the Windows VM, not in-game captures.
